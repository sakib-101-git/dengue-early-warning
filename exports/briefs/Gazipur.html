How these numbers were made

Forecast for the week of 28 Jul 2025

The forecast

Dengue Watch predicts how many dengue cases each ward will have 3 weeks ahead. It uses recent rainfall, temperature and humidity, each ward's drainage, crowding and sanitation, and the time of year. It learned from 2017 to 2022 and was checked on 2024 and 2025, where it was usually within about 1 case per ward per week. The "likely" range covers about 8 in 10 outcomes. This brief uses data up to 7 Jul 2025.

Supplies and stock

Supply needs come straight from the expected cases. Each expected case needs about 2.4 saline bags, 1.2 test kits and 0.3 extra (surge) beds; these rates were learned from 2017 to 2022 hospital records. This brief assumes the regional store holds 60% of what all 60 wards need. Critical-risk wards are served first, then the other wards in order of risk and crowding, until the stock runs out. The 40 spraying crews in the pool are shared the same way.

Hospital beds

The data has no official bed count, so each ward's regular beds are estimated as the most it needed in a bad week (the busiest 1 week in 20) during 2017 to 2022, with at least 2 beds. Bed needs are planned for a bad week (the upper end of the likely range). Patients who cannot get a regular bed are sent to the ward in the same city with the most free regular beds; wards at critical risk keep their spare beds. Distances are not known, so travel time is not considered.

Spraying crews

Each spraying crew is assumed to remove about 10% of a ward's mosquito breeding sites (standing water), up to half of them with several crews. The cases-prevented figures re-run the forecast with fewer breeding sites. They are estimates, not results from the field.

Limits

Forecasts can miss sudden outbreaks or changes in how cases are reported. Supply, bed and crew figures are planning estimates. Check local conditions before acting.
